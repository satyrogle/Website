# H2 — THE ASCENSION ENGINE, INSIDE, reference board

**Governing sentence:** the heaven above is manufactured output.

**Status:** candidate at equal standing with H1 and H3. No winner.
**Binding:** the constitution's 30 banned patterns and the section 3 equivalence
rule, the kit's H2 kill conditions, and the kill words in `CLAUDE.md`.
**Rule:** every entry names a quality to extract. Nothing here is copied, and
nothing here names a build method.

The hardest constraint on this board: the visitor is inside something enormous,
and it must never become a dome, sphere, stadium, cave, cooling tower, hangar or
planetary shell. Every reference below is screened for that first.

---

## I. INTERIOR SCALE WITHOUT A BOUNDARY

### 1. Piranesi, Carceri d'Invenzione
`Piranesi Carceri d'Invenzione plate imaginary prisons`

The canonical interior that cannot be measured: levels above levels, structures
leaving every frame edge, no wall that resolves the volume.

- **TAKE** — incomplete boundary as the source of scale; stacked levels reading
  at different distances; mass that continues past the crop in every direction.
- **REJECT** — the etched line quality, and every literal prop he draws: chains,
  wheels, winches, ropes, staircases, the prison read.
- **FRAME** — 1 and 2. This is the reference that prevents a dome.

### 2. Photomultiplier arrays in deep detectors
`Super-Kamiokande photomultiplier array wall` (ordered-unit quality only)

Thousands of identical units tiled into a surface that curves away and is never
seen whole.

- **TAKE** — a *built* surface made of thousands of ordered discrete sources;
  the way regularity at that count stops reading as pattern and starts reading
  as sky.
- **REJECT** — the gold and bronze colour, the cylindrical tank, the water, the
  boat, and every cue that says scientific facility. The circular read here is
  actively dangerous: crop so no curve completes.
- **FRAME** — 1.

### 3. Night city seen from directly above, heavy overcast
`city lights from above through cloud layer long exposure monochrome`

Ordered human light diffused by a medium, with structure implied but not shown.

- **TAKE** — a field of made light seen through depth; brightness organised into
  regions rather than scattered; the sense of a system rather than a scene.
- **REJECT** — streets, grids, recognisable geography, warm sodium colour, and
  the "city" read itself.
- **FRAME** — 1.

## II. THE LOWER WORLD — material, not machinery

### 4. Anselm Kiefer, lead and ash works
`Anselm Kiefer lead ash stratified vitrine Barjac`

Stratified poverty: ash, lead, sediment, layers pressed under something ordered.

- **TAKE** — the material vocabulary of the lower world entirely: ash, dust,
  lead grey, matte surfaces that hold no light of their own; strata as a way of
  showing history.
- **REJECT** — paint texture, canvas, the gallery, straw, any figurative or
  textual element, the German-history reading.
- **FRAME** — 5.

### 5. Flotation residue and fine tailings at macro scale
`mineral flotation froth macro monochrome` / `fine tailings surface texture`

Material that has been processed and has nothing left to give.

- **TAKE** — dead particulate matter, matte and light-absorbing; the visual
  proof that something was removed; the contrast against the pale ordered world
  above.
- **REJECT** — every piece of recognisable plant equipment, tanks, pipes,
  conveyors, and the mine or factory read the kit kills by name.
- **FRAME** — 5.

### 6. Sediment cores and varved strata
`varve sediment core layered strata monochrome`

A record written in layers, readable top to bottom.

- **TAKE** — the lower world as an archive of what rose; horizontal banding as
  accumulated time; fine, quiet, non-dramatic structure.
- **REJECT** — the core tube, the laboratory, the sample label, any scientific
  framing.
- **FRAME** — 5.

## III. THE DIRECTION OF THE WORLD

### 7. Ordered upward flow in still air
`ash column still air long exposure` / `slow rising particulate backlit monochrome`

Sparse, slow, unmistakably one-directional movement.

- **TAKE** — upward as a property of the whole space rather than of any object;
  sparseness — a few things rising, not a storm.
- **REJECT** — smoke plumes, fire, embers, sparks, anything with a source at the
  bottom of frame, and the particle-storm the constitution bans.
- **FRAME** — 1, 3 and 4.

## IV. ANTI-REFERENCES — what H2 must never become

Mandatory group.

| Anti-reference | Why it kills H2 |
|---|---|
| Dome or planetarium interior | First kill condition. Any completed curve overhead ends the direction. |
| Cooling tower interior | Kill condition. The hyperboloid is instantly nameable and very close to this composition. |
| Aircraft hangar, spaceship interior | Kill condition. Large span plus visible floor plus ordered ceiling lights lands here by default. |
| Mine or factory tour | Kill condition. Any recognisable equipment converts the reveal into an infographic. |
| Temple, ziggurat, processional stair | The levels must read as spatial function, never as ceremony architecture. Constitution bans literal sacred ornament. |
| Central engine, furnace, reactor | Kill condition. The process is distributed. A glowing middle destroys it. |
| Six themed rooms | Kill condition and constitution 4.6 equivalence. Levels differ by space, never by decoration. |

## V. WHAT THIS BOARD DECIDES

For the two frames being built now:

1. **No curve may complete.** The far interior is always cropped. The moment the
   eye can close the shape, it becomes a dome and the direction is dead.
2. **The sky is made of countable things.** Its holiness comes from order and
   number at distance, not from a glow. It must survive bloom being switched
   off, because the sources are objects.
3. **Up is a property of the composition, not of an object.** Everything leans
   the same way while almost nothing moves.
4. **The lower world absorbs light; the upper world emits it.** That single
   material inversion carries the reveal, with no new object introduced.
5. **Nothing in frame 5 may be identifiable as equipment.** If a viewer can name
   a machine, the frame is an industrial illustration and it has failed.
