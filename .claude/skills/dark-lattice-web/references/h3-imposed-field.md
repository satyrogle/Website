# H3 — THE IMPOSED FIELD, reference board

**Governing sentence:** the beautiful sky is a classification system holding
every entry in an assigned position.

**Status:** candidate at equal standing with H1 and H2. No winner.
**Binding:** the constitution's 30 banned patterns and the section 3 equivalence
rule, the kit's H3 kill conditions, and the kill words in `CLAUDE.md`.
**Rule:** every entry names a quality to extract. Nothing here is copied, and
nothing here names a build method.

This board carries the heaviest anti-reference load of the three. The field must
be material and world-scale, and it must never become a starfield, a cemetery,
an LED wall, a data visualisation or a repeated monolith. The constitution's dot
grid ban (4.23) and radial orb ban (4.22) both apply directly.

---

## I. ORDER IMPOSED ON VARIED THINGS

### 1. Core and specimen archives
`ice core archive rack storage` / `geological core library shelves`

Thousands of physical samples, each materially different, each held in an exact
assigned position. The order is administrative and it is absolute.

- **TAKE** — exact positional law holding materially varied objects; the fact
  that position carries meaning the object itself does not; the sense of a
  register rather than a collection.
- **REJECT** — shelving, racking, the warehouse, labels, barcodes, the room. Any
  visible container turns this into a facility.
- **FRAME** — 1, 2 and 5. This is the concept's spine.

### 2. Type specimens and the recorded absence
`herbarium type specimen sheet` / `museum collection gap missing specimen`

When a specimen is withdrawn, the position remains and is documented. The gap is
itself a record.

- **TAKE** — absence as evidence rather than emptiness; a hole in an order that
  is clearly deliberate; the difference between "nothing here" and "something
  removed".
- **REJECT** — paper, mounts, handwriting, the archival aesthetic entirely.
- **FRAME** — 4 and 5.

### 3. Michael Heizer, City
`Michael Heizer City Nevada earthwork geometry aerial`

Unornamented geometric order imposed at territorial scale.

- **TAKE** — imposed geometry at a size that reads as landscape; mass with no
  decoration; the refusal to explain itself.
- **REJECT** — the desert, daylight, the earthwork read, the horizon. H3 has no
  ground plane and no sky.
- **FRAME** — 5. This is what the dead lattice should feel like.

## II. THE ENTRIES — substantial, ambiguous, not repeated

### 4. Delaminated mica and muscovite sheets
`muscovite mica sheet delamination translucent edge macro`

Thin plates that have separated, with light escaping between the layers rather
than from a source.

- **TAKE** — the entry form itself: thin faceted plate, substantial, light
  escaping only at damaged interfaces; material variation within one clear type.
- **REJECT** — the mineral-specimen photograph, the collector's context, warm
  transmitted colour, anything crystalline enough to read as a gem.
- **FRAME** — 2, 3 and 4.

### 5. Fractured structural glass and laminated failure
`laminated glass fracture pattern held in place monochrome`

Something broken that has not fallen apart, still held.

- **TAKE** — internal fracture that stays contained; light finding the fracture
  and nothing else; damage as the only reason a thing is visible.
- **REJECT** — the window, the frame, the impact-point starburst, the safety
  glass read, the shattered-screen cliché.
- **FRAME** — 4.

### 6. Kiln-stacked bisque ware in a saggar
`kiln stacked bisque ware saggar packed forms`

Varied handmade forms packed to an exact stacking discipline.

- **TAKE** — related-but-not-identical units under a strict packing law; the
  tension between handmade variation and imposed spacing.
- **REJECT** — pottery, the kiln, the craft read, warmth of any kind.
- **FRAME** — 2 and 3.

## III. GRADE AND DEPTH

### 7. Deep-field layered exposure
`long exposure layered depth pale forms in black volumetric monochrome`

Forms separating into distinct depth layers by contrast alone.

- **TAKE** — genuine three-dimensional field rather than a wall; near entries
  cropped and bright, far entries dim and small; black that has scattered light
  in it rather than being empty.
- **REJECT** — stars, constellations, nebulae, any astronomical read. This is the
  single most likely failure of H3 and the kit kills it by name.
- **FRAME** — 1.

## IV. ANTI-REFERENCES — what H3 must never become

Mandatory group, and the longest of the three boards for good reason.

| Anti-reference | Why it kills H3 |
|---|---|
| Starfield, Hubble deep field | First kill condition. Small bright things on black *is* a starfield unless every entry has visible material size. |
| Cemetery, war-grave rows | Kill condition. Regular upright forms in receding rows lands here immediately. |
| Berlin Holocaust memorial | The monolith-field read the kit bans, and an unacceptable borrowing besides. |
| LED wall, teamLab, Kusama room | Kill condition. Light-emitting units in a grid, with the meaning in the installation rather than the material. |
| Node-and-edge graph, data visualisation | Kill condition, and a kill word in `CLAUDE.md`. Any visible connecting line does this instantly. |
| Dot grid | Constitution 4.23 outright. Entries must have material size and orientation, never be points. |
| Identical repeated primitive | Kill condition, and the visible-primitives lesson that killed five earlier carriers. If the eye can name the unit, the field has failed. |
| Explicit matrix lines, wireframe | Kill condition. The lattice is revealed by shadow, occlusion and support, never by drawing it. |

## V. WHAT THIS BOARD DECIDES

For the two frames being built now:

1. **Every entry must have material size, orientation and damage.** A point is a
   star. A plate with a fractured edge is an entry. This one distinction decides
   whether the direction lives.
2. **Position is exact; form is not.** The spacing obeys a law the forms do not.
   That contradiction is the whole idea and it must be visible in frame 1,
   before any copy explains it.
3. **Light only from strain, fracture or contact.** Nothing emits because it is
   pretty. No source is ever visible.
4. **The gaps are in frame 1.** Removal is not introduced at the reveal; it is
   already there and simply unread. The reveal is recognition, not addition.
5. **The lattice does not glow.** It is dead graphite, revealed by what it
   occludes and what it holds. If it has to be lit to be seen, the frame is
   drawing a diagram.
6. **No visible edge, floor or roof.** The field is cropped by the frame on every
   side, and large black voids carry the typography.
