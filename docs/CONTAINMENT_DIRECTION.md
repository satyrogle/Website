## Why v4 was superseded

Not because the planet is wrong. **The planet is in Act I and belongs in Act
III.**

v4's success condition was that a viewer understands they are travelling
through fragments of an exploding planet. That revelation is *informational*:
"these rocks came from that world." The revelation this company needs is
*ontological*:

> "Wait. This beautiful thing was doing that the entire time."

A visitor who sees a dead world in frame one has no false interpretation left
to overturn. There is no costume change available, because the costume was
never worn. v4 also banned "Abstract entities" outright, which banned the
thing the site is actually about.

The engineering is not wasted. `bc94903` is frozen as the best version of the
planet layer and is reused whole, later, where it pays off.

## Act I — OBSERVE. The angelic state.

Near-total black. No lava planet. No debris field. No orange explosion.

Something enormous occupies about a third of the frame. The visitor cannot
say what it is.

- Extraordinarily **ordered**. Order is the entire first impression; it is
  what makes the later comprehension land.
- Thin, pale, near-white energetic **trajectories**. The visible element is a
  path, not an object.
- Layered **interference** structures — trajectories crossing at different
  depths, reading as one coherent system rather than as many strands.
- Impossible curvature. Curvature no natural process and no manufacturing
  process would produce.
- A faint radial *suggestion* and no flower, no mandala, no rings. See the
  symmetry law below; it is unchanged and it is absolute.
- Regions fold inward and repeat at different scales, so the eye cannot fix
  the object's size.

It must be unclassifiable: it could be celestial, mathematical, biological or
engineered, and the visitor can commit to none of them. **The moment it is
nameable, this direction has failed** — the same law that killed the abstract
carriers, now pointed at the thing it was always about.

The planet is already in the scene at this point, behind the structure,
occluded. It is not hinted at. It is simply not visible yet.

## Act II — the mechanism. Fission as behaviour, not as an aesthetic.

The site must not *look* like nuclear energy. It must *behave* like
criticality.

The first event happens without the visitor:

1. One energetic point travels through the structure.
2. It reaches a node. Two paths leave.
3. Those reach two more. Four leave. Then eight.
4. For roughly **700 ms** the beautiful object becomes brighter and vastly
   more complicated.
5. The system arrests it.
6. Paths disappear **in reverse causal order** — last-born first, unwinding
   back to the origin. This is the tell. Nothing natural retracts along its
   own history.
7. The structure settles.
8. A microscopic **scar** remains, at the origin. Nothing explains it.

Then, and only then: **TOUCH IT.**

The visitor now produces the phenomenon themselves. It is genuinely beautiful
the first time. The sinister reading is not authored into any single event —
**it forms through repetition**, as the visitor notices the arrest is not a
property of the phenomenon but a response to it, and that the scars are
accumulating, and that something is counting.

Law: **light is only ever produced by propagation and arrest.** No decorative
energy, ever, anywhere. If a pixel is bright, a simulated event made it
bright. This is the rule that makes the site technically hard for a reason
that matters.

## Act III — the descent penetrates the structure

> **HISTORICAL from 2026-08-15. The planet is retired — see amendment 4 at the
> top.** The camera still enters the structure, and that beat is live. Nothing
> arrives inside it. The depth table and the payoff below are a record of an
> abandoned plan, not a specification.

The camera does not visit objects. It **enters the thing it was looking at**.

The bloom the visitor observed from outside becomes the mouth. As the camera
approaches, scale stops being legible: a filament that read as a hairline
becomes an enormous structure; a node that read as a point becomes something
hundreds of metres across; the camera passes *between* them.

Then the planet begins to arrive — never announced, never all at once:

| Depth | What appears |
|---|---|
| 35–40% | A strange black surface passes behind the filaments. Unidentifiable. |
| 50% | Another curved surface. A molten fracture is visible for about a second, lit only because a chain reaction happened to run past it. |
| 60% | A gigantic curved crust slab crosses the foreground. Now there is enough evidence to suspect the scale. |
| 70% | The lattice is visibly attached to, wrapped around and penetrating these structures. |

At 70% the interpretation changes. The visitor is not inside a celestial
organism. They are inside a **containment mechanism**, and the beautiful form
from frame one was its outer surface.

## The payoff

At the deepest point, the ruptured world — the frame that is currently given
away in the first second.

Slabs kilometres from their seats. The core exposed. The world dead or dying.
And nothing finishes exploding.

Because the structure the visitor just travelled through is continuously
forcing every component back toward its recorded state.

The site did not take the visitor to an exploding planet. It took them
*through the thing preventing an explosion from being permitted to complete*.
The beautiful thing from frame one was the sinister thing. There was never a
transformation — only comprehension. This satisfies the experience law in
`CLAUDE.md` exactly: divine interface, menace through understanding.
