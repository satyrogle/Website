## The one sentence

The stone remembers what was done to it, the break obeys the grain, and the
plain is full of everything that was broken before you got here.

## 1. Decision frame (constitution 12.1)

**1. Purpose.** Replace the interior act. Make the deterministic kernel visible
as a picture instead of as a statistics line, and give the descent a reason to
exist that is not "the same rock, closer".

**2. Visitor's primary task.** Look at a piece of stone, work out where it is
going to split, cause the split, and be right.

**3. What must be demonstrated.**

- A rule that is legible before it fires. The bedding says where the break goes.
- Consequence. The visitor's input produces one specific outcome, not a mess.
- Determinism as a relationship, not a single run. The same rule visibly ran many
  times before, with different results each time, and all of those results are
  standing in the frame.
- Memory. The material carries its own history in its shape.

**4. Visual mechanism.** One material seen at three scales: a plain of finished
breaks, one mass approached, and the fold itself. Warm light exists nowhere in
the world except inside a break that is still fresh. The visitor makes the only
warm light in the journey.

**5. Banned patterns most likely to appear here.** Audited in section 5. The live
ones are pale fog drifting toward white, the light source becoming decorative,
arrows as the input affordance, and the plain reading as a burial ground.

**6. Evidence required for acceptance.** Jacob looks at the frames. Nothing else
is acceptance. A frame is never defended with a number, and no measurement in
this document may be quoted back at him.

## 3. The six frames

Camera heights are relative to a standing person, because that is the only scale
the visitor owns. F1 to F5 describe locked pictures. F6 is specification.

---

### F1 OPENING. THE PLAIN. LOCKED.

**Camera.** High, roughly forty metres, looking slightly down and along. Long
lens. It frames no sky event, no horizon feature and no single mass at the centre.

**Composition.** Mass sits in the lower two thirds, scattered unevenly, never
paired, never in a row, never in a ring. The nearest mass enters from the left and
is cropped by the frame edge while still ending inside it, so it stays a member of
the population rather than becoming the terrain. Void is the upper third.
Text-safe zone is the flat fog band across the top.

**Value.** Ground and stone run near black to low mid. Fog is mid-dark grey, not
pale. There is no white anywhere and no highlight above roughly a third of the
way to white. Peak white is not spent until F4. This frame sits at its floor:
one step darker kills it.

**Material.** Wet-dark slate, matte, every mass visibly built of stacked plates.
Loose plate debris pools thickly at the base of each mass and thins across the
open ground between them, because each mass made its own. Not polished, not
metallic, not carved, not built. No snow, no ice, no frost.

**Light law.** Flat overcast. No visible source, because the sky is the source.
Every mass is cold because every mass is old.

**Scale cue.** Braided threads of track winding across the plain at footpath
width. That is the only thing stating the size, and it must stay a thread. Widen
it and the masses shrink.

**The population.** Twenty or more, back to where the fog eats them. Four distinct
outcomes carry the foreground, and they are degrees of one process rather than
different objects:

```text
BARELY WENT   tall, essentially intact, one crack down it, nothing else moved
SPLINTERED    a cluster of standing blades
COLLAPSED     plates fanned flat across the ground, almost no height left
HINGED        two halves leaning apart with a gap between them
```

**Fresh faces.** Where a mass split, the exposed face is a vertical or steeply
angled wall, the same dark stone as everything else, smoother and unweathered. The
difference is finish, not brightness. Any pale surface lying flat on top of a mass
reads as snow, and snow is weather. Weather happens to everything equally, which
destroys the claim that something did this.

**Reading order.** 1. how many of them. 2. they are all the same stuff.
3. no two went the same way.

**Continuity.** Carries the entrance world's ground, fog and grade. Open question
in section 6.

**Kill condition.** A burial ground, a stone circle, a quarry, or a photograph of
Iceland. Uneven counts, no pairing and no ring are the defence. A faint read of
concrete anti-tank blocks is present and accepted.

**Known and accepted.** The far field simplifies into small upward triangles, so
the outcome variety visible up close flattens with distance. Detail simplifies at
distance, and four passes is where a good frame starts getting tuned back into a
bad one.

### F2 APPROACH. THE WALL. LOCKED.

**Camera.** Head height, on the track, moving along it.

**Composition.** The mass fills a little over half the frame on the right and runs
off both the top and the right edges, so its full shape is never visible. There is
no silhouette and no summit. The visitor is at the foot of a wall, not looking at
a tower. The left third and the lower left are open plain and fog, and that is the
text-safe zone.

**Value.** F1's exactly.

**Material.** Bedded stone whose beds wander, vary in thickness, pinch out and
swell, and are never parallel for long. They bend, and they bend hardest where
they approach the seam. Never even courses, never a clean vertical corner, never
anything that reads as built.

**The seam.** A hairline, barely open, nothing visible inside it, wandering, full
height, off centre. Every bed arrives at the same height on both sides and pinches
thinner as it meets it. That is what makes it one mass with a weakness rather than
two rocks touching, and F4 depends on it.

**Light law.** Unchanged. Nothing has happened yet, so nothing is warm.

**Scale cue.** The track, still a thread. The fanned plate debris heaped at the
foot, at door size.

**Reading order.** 1. it is far bigger than it looked. 2. the beds bend.
3. there is one seam, and only one.

**Continuity.** Same ground, fog, grade, track and population as F1. This is the
tall nearly-intact mass from F1's foreground, the only thing on the plain that has
not finished its run. That is why the visitor walks to it and why F4 has anything
left to break.

**Kill condition.** A corridor. Two masses framing the camera on both sides at
once is fatal; the visitor goes past the others, never between two. Also dead: a
tower with a visible silhouette, and masonry.

---

### F3 DESCENT. THE FOLD. LOCKED.

**Camera.** Against the wall, near square on, slightly low.

**Composition.** The face runs off the left, right and top edges. The bottom of
the frame includes the foot of the wall meeting the ground, with fallen plates at
door size heaped against it. The seam runs top to bottom about a third in from the
right.

**Value.** Near black, with raking highlights on slab edges. No highlight above
roughly halfway to white. Peak white stays unspent.

**Material.** Chunky slabs with visible ends that stop, step and overlap.
Thickness varies strongly, so no run of even courses forms anywhere. Never fibres,
never strands, never brushed fine detail.

**The fold.** Asymmetric. Long and gentle on the left, sweeping up across the full
width; short and steep on the right. Never a mirror. There is a fallen slab at the
foot of the seam, which says this seam has already shed something.

**Light law.** Overcast raking from one side, so slab edges catch and flats fall
away dark.

**Scale cue.** The fallen plates at the foot, door sized. Sand-grain grit was
tried twice and failed twice; it is not the cue and will not be asked for again.

**Reading order.** 1. slabs. 2. they bend. 3. they converge right there.

**Continuity.** The same seam as F2, at arm's length.

**Kill condition.** If the visitor cannot point at the seam before being told, the
frame has failed and the whole premise fails with it. This is the load-bearing
frame. Also dead: hair, fur, strands or brushed fibre; masonry; a feather or a
leaf; a material swatch with nothing stating its size.

---

### F4 THE PRESS AND THE DISCREPANCY. LOCKED.

**Camera.** Identical to F3. It does not move, shake or pull back. The stone does
the work.

**Composition.** Identical to F3. Only the rock changes.

**The split.** Opens along the exact seam from F3, following the bending. Nothing
sprays, nothing scatters, nothing flies. The fallen plates at the foot are exactly
where they were.

**Value.** The only peak white in the journey. Near white at the narrowest hottest
points, falling to deep orange along the length, uneven and broken rather than a
continuous stripe.

**Light law.** The heat throws light onto the stone within a hand's width either
side of the seam, brightest where the seam is widest and falling to nothing beyond
it. It must read as light coming out of the rock, never as a line drawn on the
rock. The rest of the face stays dark and cold.

**The discrepancy.** A second seam roughly a hand's width to the left, parallel,
following the same bending, completely closed and healed over, its edges rounded,
cold and unlit. Plainly the same kind of seam, only old. New and hot beside old
and cold in a single look, which is the two-runs comparison arriving at the size
of one stone. Nothing points at it and nothing explains it.

**Reading order.** 1. it went where I thought it would. 2. there is light in
there. 3. there is another one, and it is old.

**Continuity.** Identical camera to F3. That is what proves the split was caused
rather than cut to.

**Kill condition.** Spray, scatter or flying debris. Lava, neon, or a glowing
crack used as a brand device. A uniform warm stripe with no falloff.

**Known and accepted.** The beds between the two seams form a soft V. Weaker than
the feather that was killed at F3, and broken up by having two seams. The old seam
reads slightly pale, close to a mineral vein; accepted, because pale is what makes
the comparison readable.

### F5 REVEAL. WHAT THE PLAIN IS. LOCKED.

**Camera.** Pulled back and risen off the wall, to roughly between F2 and F1.

**Composition.** The mass the visitor split is whole in frame and no larger than
its neighbours. Masses at several distances back into the fog. The track winds
through. Collapsed low masses and plate debris in the foreground.

**Value.** F1's darkness exactly. Near-black stone, mid-dark fog, no pale areas
anywhere.

**Material.** Folded irregular stone, beds wandering and varying. Never blocks,
courses, steps, cairns, or anything that looks piled or built.

**Light law.** One thin warm thread running down a seam on one mass, near white at
a couple of points, deep orange along its length. Every other mass on the plain is
cold and dark with no light in it at all. That thread is the only warm colour in
the frame.

**Scale cue.** The track.

**Reading order.** 1. mine is one of them now. 2. it is the only warm one.
3. the others are cold because the others are finished.

**Continuity.** Same world, ground, fog, grade and track as F1.

**Kill condition.** Anything added to the frame to explain the reveal: a label, a
count, a UI element, a monster. The reveal is entirely comprehension.

**Known and accepted.** The standing masses converge on a leaning fin silhouette.
Raised twice, not rolled a third time. The foreground collapses carry the variety.

### F6 RETURN. LOCKED.

**Correction to this board.** F6 was originally written as "identical to F1 plus
one more mass". That is wrong. The visitor breaks the nearly-intact mass that was
already standing in F1's foreground, so the plain holds exactly the same number of
things at the end as at the start. Nothing is added. One thing changed state, and
it is the one the visitor touched.

That is a stronger return than a new object appearing, because the count is
unchanged and the visitor has to notice.

**Camera.** F1's exactly. Not similar, identical.

**Method.** Composited from the locked F1 by `tools/f6-composite.py`, never
regenerated. Fixed seed, no clocks, so re-running produces byte-identical output.
The chosen variant is the dimmest of three: peak colour `(168, 92, 40)`, glow gain
`1.00`.

The line is drawn along the crack already present in that mass, traced at full
resolution: `(167,198) (178,272) (189,348) (199,424) (209,498) (219,562)
(227,608)`. Its brightness is uneven and broken along its length rather than a
uniform stripe, the crack is deepened slightly so the light sits inside a real
opening, and a small blurred halo puts local falloff on the stone either side.
Every other pixel in the frame is F1 untouched.

**The one change.** The tall nearly-intact mass on the left is now split along its
crack, with a thin cooling line of deep orange in the opening. It is becoming one
of the old ones.

**Value, measured.** F5's hottest warm pixel is `rgb(210,168,122)`. F6's is
`rgb(150,110,82)`. Dimmer than F5, far below white, and F4 keeps the only peak
white in the journey. This is the one place a number is recorded, because the
claim is "fainter than the frame before it" and that is checkable rather than
aesthetic.

**Everything else.** Identical. Same fog, same track, same masses in the same
positions, same grade. Any other difference is a bug.

**Kill condition.** If F6 is not literally F1's frame, the return does not land and
the act ends on a shrug, which is the current act's ending problem.

**Why generation was abandoned.** Three attempts, and every one produced a new
camera and a second warm mass. Two of them made the same mistake in a row after
being told. A generator has no access to the approved picture, so it cannot
return to it. The brush can.

## 4. The rules that hold the whole board together

1. **Warm light is only ever the inside of a fresh break.** Nothing else in the
   world is warm. This is why the visitor's action is the only warm thing that has
   ever been in the frame, and it is free.
2. **Every mass is a finished run.** Same material, same rule, different outcome.
   That is determinism shown as a population instead of asserted as a number.
3. **The break obeys the bedding.** If it is arbitrary, there is nothing to
   predict and the act reverts to exploding blocks.
4. **No corridor, ever.** The visitor goes past masses, never between two.
5. **The camera never performs.** It holds still at the press. Movement is travel,
   never reaction.
6. **Nothing is added to explain anything.** No label, no count, no stats line.
7. **Never say layered, blocky or stratified.** Those three words produced masonry
   three separate times: even courses at F2, brick staggering at F3, stepped
   ziggurats and a ruined civilisation at F5. The words that work describe
   behaviour rather than structure: folded, wandering, bending, pinching out,
   varying in thickness, never parallel for long.
8. **Composite rather than regenerate when a frame must match a locked one.**
   Established at F6. Re-rolling loses the thing that was approved.
9. **When a note fails twice, change the method, not the wording.** Scale failed
   twice as sand grit and was solved by putting the foot of the wall in frame. The
   healed break failed twice inside the opening and was solved by moving it onto
   the face. Both fixes were better than what they replaced.

**Gate B, disguised equivalents.**

1. Replaced a banned pattern with an equivalent? The tunnel is refused outright
   rather than disguised. Rule 4 stops it returning as a walk between two masses,
   and the F2 kill condition enforces it.
2. Resembles a common template? The live risks are a landscape photograph, land
   art, and ruins of a lost civilisation. All three named, all three defended by
   uneven counts, cropping, and rule 7.
3. Any element present only because sites usually have it? No.
4. Effects hiding weak information architecture? Not yet testable. F2 and F3 carry
   the text-safe zones and that is where the editorial load lands.
5. Generic motion as polish? Rule 5 forbids it.
6. Trendy font or palette without a reason? No type chosen. Palette is one
   material and one event, both derived from Jacob's images.

**Gate C, evidence.** The board's central claim is that the kernel is
deterministic, and its method is to show that rather than state it. The stats line
is deleted. No frame asserts anything not visible in it.

## 6. Open questions for Jacob, not assumptions

1. **The spire.** The exterior monument the visitor comes from could be one of the
   masses on the plain. That turns "the inside is a mimic of the spire" from the
   complaint into the mechanism: the outside was one run, the plain is all of them.
   Recommended, not assumed.
2. **Where the company copy lives.** F2's open left third and F3's flat lower face
   are the two long still surfaces. That is one stop each, and the oldest open
   wound on this project is that there is no home for company information and
   contact. This board does not solve that. It only names the shelves.
3. **The input.** The press has to be caused, not scrubbed. The current three
   socket detent is a scrub, and its arrow affordance already fails 4.25. Not
   designed here.
4. **The ending.** F6 is a real landing beat, which the current act does not have.
   It resolves item 2 on the ranked better-list.
