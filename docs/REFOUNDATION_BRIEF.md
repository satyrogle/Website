# DARK LATTICE WEBSITE — REFOUNDATION DESIGN BRIEF v1.0

**Status:** Fresh start / replacement source of truth  
**Company:** Dark Lattice Ltd  
**Stack:** Vite + TypeScript + Three.js 0.170 + WebGL2/GLSL + GSAP/ScrollTrigger  
**Excluded unless explicitly approved:** React, React Three Fiber, Drei, Tailwind, shadcn, Lenis, decorative plugin layers

---

## 1. Executive decision

This is not another revision of the planet, ribbon, choir, wave, or static-carrier experiments. It is a controlled restart.

The new website must be built around one visible mechanism:

> **A beautiful system accepts the visitor’s input, appears to cooperate, then reveals that it has been judging, correcting, and reorganising that input according to rules the visitor did not initially understand.**

The website must not be a portfolio with a cinematic object behind it. The visual object, interaction, copy, project evidence, navigation, and final reveal must all be different expressions of the same system.

The current planet/debris branch should be archived as an experiment, not treated as a base that must be rescued.

---

## 2. What the previous iterations proved

### What worked

- The earliest **DMT bloom / tunnel / entity** direction created the strongest emotional response: beauty, scale, mystery, and the sense that something intelligent existed deeper inside.
- The locked philosophical progression remains correct: **angelic and controlled first; sinister only after comprehension**.
- The concept of a persistent simulation and a correction mechanism is stronger than any isolated 3D object.
- A brutally dark field with concentrated, physically convincing energy is more appropriate than generic luxury-site polish.
- The existing Vite, TypeScript, Three.js, GLSL, and GSAP stack is sufficient.

### What failed

1. **Form replaced mechanism.** Ribbon, curtains, waves, and the planet became carriers waiting to be made attractive. The visitor could not understand why the form existed.
2. **The real interaction became invisible.** The correction, deviation, pulse, and adjustment systems existed, but the press path was disabled. The site became a static GLB while the meaningful system ran behind it.
3. **Blender work began before the screen experience was proven.** Considerable effort went into topology, wounds, slabs, materials, and debris before the hero composition and interaction had passed a simple visual test.
4. **The planet made the idea too literal.** It drifted toward familiar cinematic destruction, shell peeling, implosion, glowing-core imagery, and generic space spectacle.
5. **Scroll became transport rather than consequence.** Moving the camera from debris to debris did not inherently explain Dark Lattice.
6. **Bloom and emissive treatment were asked to rescue weak structure.** The design must still work as a silhouette and value composition with bloom disabled.
7. **Content and experience were separated.** Text appeared to sit on top of a scene rather than being generated by the same logic.
8. **There was no enforced proof gate.** Large implementation branches were allowed to continue after the central image or behavior had already failed.

## 3. Brand position the site must communicate

Dark Lattice is a technology product company using game-design methods—systems, interaction, feedback, progression, simulation, consequence, and emergence—to build games and broader software products.

The public site should present the company as:

- technically serious;
- visually authored rather than template-led;
- systems-first rather than spectacle-first;
- dark without becoming juvenile horror;
- experimental without becoming unreadable;
- capable of explaining and evidencing its work.

### Primary public proposition

> **Dark Lattice builds games and software where rules, evidence, and consequence shape the experience.**

### Supporting line

> **Technology products designed through game-systems thinking.**

No unsupported superlatives, fake metrics, fake clients, invented awards, or vague “we redefine the future” language.

---

## 4. Audience and visitor jobs

### Primary audiences

1. Endorsers, advisors, investors, and commercial partners assessing whether the company is coherent and credible.
2. Skilled collaborators assessing whether Dark Lattice has a real design and production philosophy.
3. Players and interested visitors discovering Desk42 and Project Brawler.

### What each visitor must be able to do

- Understand what Dark Lattice builds within the first screen.
- Experience one meaningful interaction within the first 15–20 seconds.
- Reach real project information without completing an intro sequence.
- See evidence that the visual philosophy is connected to actual products.
- Contact the company without hunting through the 3D experience.

---

## 5. Core experiential arc

The experience uses one persistent state machine across the full homepage.

### State 0 — Dormant order

The system appears perfect, balanced, luminous, and nearly sacred. The page is quiet. Nothing reads as damaged or evil.

### State 1 — Contact

The field acknowledges the pointer, keyboard, or touch with precise local response. The visitor believes the system is cooperative.

### State 2 — Intervention

The visitor is asked to press and hold, align, stabilise, recognise, or correct one state. The action must be simple and legible.

### State 3 — Correction

The system accepts the action, then moves beyond it. A small but undeniable discrepancy appears. The first action was not control; it was evidence.

### State 4 — Exposure

As the visitor explores the work, the same discrepancy propagates through the visual field, typography, project evidence, and motion. The visitor understands that the system is classifying and rewriting conditions.

### State 5 — Comprehension

The final view does not reveal a monster. It reveals the logic that was present from the beginning. Earlier beauty is reinterpreted as containment, filtering, or institutional order.

### State 6 — Release

The website returns control: direct links to projects, company information, and contact. The experience ends in clarity, not a vague cinematic fade.

---

## 6. Interaction rules

- The first meaningful input must visibly affect the central system immediately.
- Every visual response must be traceable to input, simulation state, or scroll progress.
- Scroll may advance state, but it must not hijack, smooth over, or obscure wheel-input causality.
- The visitor can use normal navigation at any time.
- No random ambient particle motion presented as intelligence.
- No interaction whose only effect is camera parallax.
- The system should preserve a compact record of the visitor’s intervention during the session so the final state is genuinely derived from what happened earlier.
- Keyboard and touch must provide equivalent actions.
- Reduced-motion mode must preserve the conceptual reveal through discrete state changes.

---

## 7. Content architecture

### 01 — Hero / proposition

- Dark Lattice mark and name.
- Direct statement of what the company builds.
- One thematic line.
- Two actions: **Enter the system** and **View work**.
- The central interactive mechanism.

### 02 — Method / systems thesis

Explain the company through three concrete principles:

- rules create behavior;
- decisions leave evidence;
- consequences persist.

This section should not be a row of cards. Each principle should appear as a state change in the persistent visual system, paired with concise DOM text.

### 03 — Work

Two principal project entries:

- **Desk42** — institutional reality, official records, reliance, precedent, and consequences.
- **Project Brawler** — deterministic reactive contamination and gameplay consequences.

Each project gets one unmistakable visual behavior, one sentence explaining the product, and one evidence-led detail. Full information lives on dedicated project pages.

### 04 — Production system

Present the six production labs as one connected pipeline, not six disconnected feature cards:

DesignLab → ArtLab → AudioLab → ContentLab → QALab → ReleaseLab.

The visual system should show output moving through constraints and validation rather than simply listing departments.

### 05 — Company

- Company thesis.
- Founder roles.
- Current collaborators where appropriate.
- Location and company status.
- No inflated team presentation.

### 06 — Contact

A clean, immediate endpoint with email and relevant professional links. The visual system settles into its final exposed state behind the contact block.

## 8. Visual design system

### Overall image

- 80–90% near-black negative space.
- One central field, aperture, disc, or plane—not multiple hero objects.
- Light appears physically concentrated rather than sprayed across the screen.
- Beauty comes from proportion, symmetry, controlled energy, and exact timing.
- The sinister layer appears through asymmetry, occlusion, hysteresis, accumulated deviation, and irreversible state.

### Palette

- **Void:** `#020304`
- **Carbon:** `#080B0E`
- **Bone white:** `#E9EEF2`
- **Peak white:** `#F8FBFF`
- **Cherenkov blue:** `#56D8FF`
- **Deep energy blue:** `#1546D8`
- **Violation red:** `#FF493D` used very rarely and never as a broad gradient

Avoid purple cyberpunk gradients, radioactive green clichés, caramel/orange crust, rainbow chromatic aberration, and continuous molten rings.

### Typography

- One disciplined display grotesk for brand and headings.
- One highly legible text face for explanatory copy.
- Optional mono only for evidence, state, or measurement labels.
- Large, readable type; no tiny sci-fi annotations used as decoration.
- Text remains real DOM content, selectable and accessible.

### Layout

- Restrained editorial grid over a full-viewport visual field.
- The hero mechanism occupies approximately 55–70% of the desktop composition.
- Headline and proposition remain anchored rather than orbiting the object.
- No bento grid, floating glass panels, excessive rounded cards, or fake control-room UI.
- Mobile becomes a clear linear document with a reduced but still stateful visual layer.

### Motion

- Slow tension, fast correction.
- Motion should have inertia only when the simulated system has inertia.
- No endless idle loops except minimal breathing caused by an explicit simulation variable.
- Camera movement remains restrained; most transformation happens in the system itself.
- Bloom, halation, and spectral effects are consequences of luminance, not a general filter.

### Sound

- Muted by default.
- Optional sound uses a low harmonic bed and short state-confirmation events.
- Sound must communicate contact, threshold, correction, and exposure; it must not be generic cinematic ambience.

# 9. HIGH-END DESIGN DIRECTION 01 — THE CRITICAL BLOOM

**Recommendation:** Primary direction to prototype first.

## Concept

Recover the emotional power of the original DMT bloom/tunnel/entity idea, but rebuild it as a deterministic critical field rather than psychedelic decoration.

A luminous radial bloom sits in the void. At first it appears perfectly balanced: part halo, part flower, part precision instrument. Its repeated structures extend backwards into a shallow tunnel, suggesting depth without becoming a literal portal.

The bloom is not an asset. It is the visible output of the site’s simulation.

## Desktop hero composition

- Dark Lattice mark at top left.
- Restrained navigation at top right.
- Large radial bloom slightly right of centre.
- Headline anchored lower left:

  **DARK LATTICE**  
  **Systems that remember what you did.**

- Practical subtitle:

  **Games and software shaped by rules, evidence, and consequence.**

- Primary action: **Hold to align**.
- Secondary action: **View work**.

## First interaction

The visitor presses and holds. The bloom’s filaments align toward the input point and the central aperture becomes clean and angelic. On release, the system performs a stronger correction than requested. One region remains offset by a few degrees. A thin Cherenkov-blue fault persists.

The visitor’s action has become a stored deviation.

## Section progression

1. **Method:** each company principle changes the rule governing the bloom—symmetry, propagation, memory.
2. **Desk42:** the visible bloom shows the official state while a delayed inner lattice shows the consequence state.
3. **Brawler:** a reaction front propagates through selected filaments using a deterministic contamination field.
4. **Production labs:** energy passes through six constrained bands, with validation gates visibly rejecting or transforming output.
5. **Company:** the tunnel depth collapses and exposes the full lattice governing every earlier state.

## Final reveal

The central “entity” is not a face or creature. It is a negative-space operator formed by all accumulated corrections. The visitor recognises that the halo was a containment and classification structure from the first frame.

Suggested final line:

> **The system did not follow your input. It used it to decide what the world could become.**

Then the page resolves into direct project and contact links.

## Technical construction

- Full-screen WebGL2 field with a small number of instanced radial bands.
- GLSL deformation driven by a deterministic fixed-step state kernel.
- Optional ping-pong render targets for reaction or trail state.
- Selective bloom only on high-luminance filaments.
- Existing correction/deviation concepts can be reused, but the input path must be live and integration-tested.
- Minimal or zero Blender dependency for the prototype.
- GSAP maps page progression to state parameters; it does not independently animate decorative properties.

## Why it avoids earlier failures

- It restores the only direction that already produced the desired awe.
- The central form exists because of the mechanism.
- Interaction is visible immediately.
- It avoids a literal planet, debris management, and expensive GLB iteration.
- The final reveal reinterprets the beginning instead of replacing it.

## Main risk

It can become a music visualiser or generic portal. Kill it immediately if the bloom cannot be read as a controlled system with real content attached.

# 10. HIGH-END DESIGN DIRECTION 02 — THE BLACK SUN PROTOCOL

## Concept

A fixed black disc occupies the centre of the page, surrounded by a razor-thin white corona and precise magnetic or reaction field lines. It reads initially as celestial and sacred, but not as a planet or conventional black hole.

The disc is an occlusion device: it hides the system’s interior while presenting a controlled boundary.

## Desktop hero composition

- Black disc centred slightly above the visual midpoint.
- White corona with sparse Cherenkov-blue regions.
- No star field, orange accretion disc, nebula, or space dust.
- Large headline aligned left:

  **CONTROL IS A TEMPORARY CONDITION.**

- Practical company proposition directly below it.
- Interaction label placed on the disc boundary: **Regulate**.

## First interaction

Dragging along the corona appears to stabilise the field lines. The outer display becomes cleaner while invisible internal criticality rises. The disc’s edge develops a subtle delay: the visual boundary reports stability after the actual field has crossed a threshold.

## Section progression

- The camera remains almost fixed.
- Content sections alter field topology, density, and occlusion rather than flying through space.
- Desk42 is expressed through two conflicting field solutions: recorded and actual.
- Brawler is expressed through a deterministic front crossing the hidden boundary.
- Production labs appear as six control stages around the circumference, but never as floating UI cards.

## Final reveal

The black disc contracts, not as an explosion, but as an aperture closing. The previously hidden interior becomes visible for a moment: a dense lattice of control paths, accumulated interventions, and suppressed alternatives.

The sinister truth is that the beautiful corona existed because the system was discarding unstable possibilities.

## Technical construction

- Signed-distance-field disc and edge treatment.
- Shader-generated corona and controlled volumetric line field.
- Instanced curves or screen-space field tracing.
- No planet mesh and no macro debris.
- Strongest design for a restrained camera and low asset weight.

## Why it avoids earlier failures

- Gives the site a single unforgettable silhouette.
- Preserves the nuclear/celestial energy without returning to destroyed-planet imagery.
- Keeps content readable because composition stays stable.
- Can be tested as a still frame before any complex build.

## Main risk

It can become a generic eclipse or black-hole website. It must be designed as an instrument and rule boundary, with no conventional space imagery.

# 11. HIGH-END DESIGN DIRECTION 03 — THE WITNESS FIELD

## Concept

A near-black plane carries an immaculate lattice of luminous lines. The surface appears flat, perfect, and authoritative. Pointer movement creates a temporary path that is immediately absorbed into the official pattern.

A second, initially invisible field records what actually happened.

This direction translates Dark Lattice’s “reality versus official reality” philosophy directly into the website rather than symbolising it through a cosmic object.

## Desktop hero composition

- Oblique luminous plane crossing the viewport from lower left to upper right.
- Sparse white linework with controlled blue energy at intersections.
- Headline:

  **THE RECORD IS NOT THE REALITY.**

- Practical proposition underneath.
- Interaction: **Mark a path**.

## First interaction

The visitor draws or drags a short path. The visible plane straightens it into the nearest permitted route. A faint displaced trace remains underneath, visible only when the surface tilts.

The page has recorded both the official result and the actual intervention.

## Section progression

- **Method:** rules for recognition, denial, reliance, and persistence are introduced visually.
- **Desk42:** the two fields become explicitly legible as official record and consequence state.
- **Brawler:** contamination crosses the lower field while the upper field continues reporting clean structure.
- **Production labs:** each lab transforms evidence before the next stage receives it.
- **Company:** the camera rotates behind the plane, revealing the full density of displaced traces that supported the immaculate front surface.

## Final reveal

The “angelic” front was a projection maintained by continuously moving irregularity to the hidden side. The surface does not break. The visitor simply gains the position required to see what was always there.

## Technical construction

- Subdivided plane with custom vertex/fragment shaders.
- Deterministic path recording and dual-field state.
- FBO or texture-backed trail accumulation.
- DOM copy aligned to a strict editorial grid.
- Lowest dependency on 3D asset production and easiest route to a strong mobile adaptation.

## Why it avoids earlier failures

- Directly expresses the company’s actual design philosophy.
- Uses interaction as evidence rather than decoration.
- Avoids portal, planet, ribbon, curtain, and creature readings.
- Offers the clearest connection between website behavior and Desk42.

## Main risk

It is less immediately cosmic and can become austere. The light, depth, sound, and typography must carry sufficient scale and awe.

## 12. Direction scorecard

| Direction | Emotional fit | Originality | Company clarity | Build risk | Mobile viability | Decision |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| The Critical Bloom | 5/5 | 5/5 | 4/5 | Medium | Strong with reduced mode | Prototype first |
| The Black Sun Protocol | 5/5 | 4/5 | 4/5 | Medium | Strong | Reserve direction |
| The Witness Field | 4/5 | 5/5 | 5/5 | Low–Medium | Strongest | Strategic fallback |

These directions must not be merged into one scene. Combining bloom, black sun, and witness plane would recreate the earlier problem of adding forms before proving one mechanism.

---

## 13. Recommended decision

Prototype **The Critical Bloom** first.

Reason:

1. It returns to the only visual territory that already produced the correct emotional response.
2. It can reuse the strongest conceptual work from THE CORRECTION without preserving the failed carrier.
3. It requires no major Blender production before proof.
4. It can embody Dark Lattice’s full philosophical arc through behavior.
5. It gives the studio a visual identity that is neither a generic luxury site nor a familiar destroyed-world scene.

Keep **The Witness Field** as the fallback if the bloom fails the “music visualiser / portal” kill test. Keep **The Black Sun Protocol** as a separate reserve concept, not an ingredient.

## 14. Proof-first production plan

### Gate A — Reference extraction

- Recover the strongest frames from the original DMT bloom/tunnel/entity version.
- Identify exactly five qualities that made them work: silhouette, depth, luminance structure, scale, and implied intelligence.
- Build one restrained physics/material board: solar corona, Cherenkov glow, magnetic field topology, reaction fronts.

**Pass:** references describe form and behavior, not just mood.  
**Fail:** the board becomes a collection of unrelated dark sci-fi images.

### Gate B — Static styleframes

Create one desktop and one mobile styleframe for each of the three directions using real Dark Lattice copy.

**Pass:** the frame looks authored and high-end with bloom disabled.  
**Fail:** it reads as a planet, portal, AI visualiser, game menu, luxury template, or generic WebGL demo.

### Gate C — Eight-second interaction proof

For the chosen direction, build only:

- dormant state;
- pointer contact;
- press/hold intervention;
- visible correction;
- one persistent deviation.

No full page, no project sections, no Blender asset expansion.

**Pass:** a viewer can explain what their input caused.  
**Fail:** the response is merely attractive motion.

### Gate D — Real-content proof

Place the actual proposition, Desk42 entry, Brawler entry, and contact path into the prototype.

**Pass:** content and mechanism strengthen each other.  
**Fail:** text looks overlaid, hidden, or secondary to the canvas.

### Gate E — Technical proof

- Desktop target: stable 60 fps on a representative mid-range machine.
- Mobile target: stable reduced path at 30–60 fps depending on device tier.
- Cap device pixel ratio.
- Defer non-critical project assets.
- Validate reduced-motion and no-WebGL fallback.
- Test the first-action path automatically so it cannot silently become disabled again.

### Gate F — Full implementation

Only after A–E pass:

- complete homepage state progression;
- build project detail pages;
- add optional sound;
- add production-lab section;
- perform accessibility, performance, cross-browser, and content audits.

## 15. Non-negotiable kill tests

Stop the direction immediately if any of the following is true:

- The hero can be described as “a cool 3D object with text.”
- The first input has no visible consequence.
- The mechanism cannot be explained in one sentence.
- The design needs particles, stars, chromatic noise, or aggressive bloom to feel alive.
- The text becomes tiny, peripheral, or unreadable.
- The mobile version is merely a video or broken desktop crop.
- The sinister phase is achieved by showing a monster, skull, demon, or gore.
- The final reveal introduces a new idea instead of reinterpreting the first frame.
- A large Blender asset is commissioned before the interaction proof passes.
- Scroll smoothing obscures direct input-to-response causality.

---

## 16. Technical architecture requirements

Use a strict separation of responsibilities:

- **ExperienceState:** canonical state machine and session-derived visitor evidence.
- **SimulationKernel:** deterministic, fixed-step logic.
- **InputController:** pointer, touch, keyboard, and press/hold commands.
- **ScrollDirector:** maps document progress to target state; does not own independent visual logic.
- **SceneRenderer:** renders snapshots of state; never invents behavior locally.
- **ContentController:** exposes the same state through accessible DOM content.
- **EvidenceRecorder:** stores the small set of session interactions required for the final reveal.
- **QualityHarness:** deterministic captures, first-action integration test, performance measurements, and reduced-motion checks.

Repository truth remains authoritative. No framework migration is part of this brief.

---

## 17. Performance and accessibility acceptance criteria

- Largest Contentful Paint target: 2.5 seconds or less at the 75th percentile.
- Interaction to Next Paint target: 200 milliseconds or less.
- Cumulative Layout Shift target: 0.1 or less.
- All key copy and navigation exist in the DOM.
- Canvas content is supplementary to, not a replacement for, company information.
- Full keyboard navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` route with equivalent narrative states.
- No autoplay audio.
- No forced intro.
- Graceful static fallback when WebGL is unavailable.

## 19. Final design principle

> **Do not build a beautiful object and search for meaning afterward. Build a rule whose visible behavior becomes beautiful, then let the visitor discover what that rule was doing to them.**
