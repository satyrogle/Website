# Dark Lattice — Three Hybrid Visual Journeys and Claude Execution Kit

**Status:** Art-direction candidates. No winner is locked.  
**Authority:** Jacob is the final visual authority.  
**Current rule:** Image first. Motion second. Technology last.

---

# 0. Shared law for all three hybrids

All three directions must preserve the same Dark Lattice emotional architecture:

1. The opening appears cosmic, immaculate, controlled, and almost holy.
2. The visitor moves inward or downward through the same world.
3. The world becomes more legible as scale changes.
4. The first discrepancy is visible before it is explained.
5. The reveal introduces no monster, demon, glowing core, or unrelated second asset.
6. The darker truth is produced by understanding what the opening was already showing.
7. The return frame remains beautiful, but the visitor can no longer read it innocently.

Holiness must come from scale, silence, order, light, and spatial authority. Do not use literal angels, halos, crosses, temples, runes, occult symbols, or fantasy-cathedral ornament.

The world must not become:

- a planet, orb, eclipse, portal, or circular tunnel;
- a mining site, factory tour, bridge, data visualisation, starfield, cemetery, LED installation, or generic sci-fi hangar;
- one cool 3D object beside company copy;
- a particle, cellular-automata, shader, or simulation demonstration presented as art direction;
- a normal landing page hidden behind cinematic fog.

Every frame must work in grayscale. The opening must still work with bloom and fog disabled. Atmosphere may reveal scale, but it may not rescue weak geometry or composition.

# H1 — THE LOAD

## Governing sentence

**Light is the visible cost of holding the structure together.**

Nothing shines by nature. Every luminous seam, face, and contact region is under strain. The visitor initially reads the light as sanctity. Deeper in, the same light becomes evidence of pressure, damage, and approaching failure.

## Why it belongs to Dark Lattice

The entire reinterpretation comes from one rule that exists in the opening frame. No second object is needed. No late horror styling is needed. The most beautiful region is also the most endangered region.

This is the strongest of the three as a total conceptual inversion.

## World architecture

A horizonless load-bearing world fills black space. It is not a bridge, tower, cliff, mine, cathedral, or machine that can be named in one glance.

The visible world is made from:

- colossal graphite and carbon masses;
- interrupted terraces and suspended support planes;
- enormous contact surfaces;
- hard voids between load paths;
- restrained bone-white luminosity only where forces accumulate;
- real occlusion and distance falloff;
- no visible outer edge;
- no visible roof, floor, or complete silhouette.

The opening should suggest that the visitor is seeing only one local portion of something vastly larger.

## Six-frame journey

### Frame 1 — Shallow load

The frame is mostly black. A colossal ordered structure occupies the right and upper regions, while the left remains a true text-safe void.

Bone-white light appears along sparse contact seams and compression faces. The structure looks serene because the load is light here. It must read as immense and intentional, not as a glowing rock or architecture render.

The company proposition appears once, plainly, in the negative space.

### Frame 2 — The first pressure path

The visitor approaches one luminous contact region. Parallax reveals that the lights are not decoration. Each light belongs to a continuous path through overlapping masses.

A single bounded visitor action may later apply or redistribute load. The affected route becomes slightly brighter and remains identifiable. During art direction, only storyboard this. Do not implement it yet.

### Frame 3 — Descent through pressure

The camera descends through dark shelves and voids between structural masses.

Each content stop sits in real negative space. Typography never sits over the main luminous subject. As depth increases, the structure becomes brighter because more load is being transferred through fewer regions.

The world becomes more beautiful as the danger increases.

### Frame 4 — The discrepancy

Close surfaces reveal compression, slight bowing, delamination, stress whitening, and hairline separation at interfaces. Avoid generic glowing cracks.

Some dark masses are not peaceful. They are unloaded because they have already stopped carrying anything.

The visitor begins to understand that brightness is not purity. It is effort.

### Frame 5 — The failure basin

The descent reaches the most luminous region in the entire world.

This is not a glowing core. It is a broad structural territory where multiple load paths converge. Surfaces are warped, interfaces are opening, and the brightest light exists at the exact boundary of failure.

There is no explosion. The structure is still holding.

The visitor-created pressure path arrives here and is visibly part of the load.

### Frame 6 — Return

The camera returns to the opening scale.

The structure remains beautiful. The light has not changed colour. The visitor now recognises every bright seam as accumulated strain.

One additional pressure path remains from the visitor’s action. The world remembers.

## Website content architecture

- **Opening:** Dark Lattice proposition and direct View Work route.
- **First descent:** What Dark Lattice builds.
- **Desk42:** A clean official structural state beside the actual retained strain history. The evidence must come from real project material, not invented diagrams.
- **Brawler:** One local failure redistributes load through neighbouring regions.
- **Labs:** Use sequential spatial levels, not six cards. Each level can introduce one stage of production without turning the world into a factory infographic.
- **Company and contact:** The visual motion calms and the page becomes more editorial near the return.

## Interaction proposition

One visitor action applies a bounded load or changes one support relationship.

The result must:

- be immediate;
- propagate visibly;
- remain stored;
- reappear in the failure basin;
- survive the return frame;
- have keyboard and touch equivalents;
- have a reduced-motion state sequence.

## Visual grammar

- Resting material: graphite, carbon, ash, bone.
- Resting light: restrained cool white.
- Cyan: only the visitor-created deviation, if needed.
- Violet: only a real correction action, if used.
- No ambient neon.
- No orange industrial lighting.
- No decorative glowing cracks.
- Fog exists only to reveal depth and load scale.

## Automatic kill conditions

Kill H1 if:

- it reads as a bridge, cliff, crystal cave, cathedral, or abstract glowing architecture;
- light looks decorative rather than structurally caused;
- the failure basin becomes a reactor core or explosion;
- the structure needs fog or bloom to feel monumental;
- the visitor’s action does not alter the meaning of the return;
- content appears pasted over a cinematic background.

# H2 — THE ASCENSION ENGINE, INSIDE

## Governing sentence

**The heaven above is manufactured output.**

The visitor arrives inside an enormous hollow world whose far interior surface appears like a constructed sky. Every compositional force points upward. The visitor descends against that direction and discovers the process that makes the immaculate world overhead.

## Why it belongs to Dark Lattice

This is the strongest spatial website architecture. It naturally turns descent into navigation and gives each level a real place to hold company content.

It also recovers the scale and inward journey Jacob liked in Igloo without using a visible tunnel, orb, planet, portal, or hero sculpture.

## World architecture

The visitor is already inside something too large to comprehend.

The far side curves away high overhead, but the complete curve is never visible. It must not read as:

- a dome;
- a sphere interior;
- a stadium;
- a cave;
- a cooling tower;
- a hangar;
- a planetary shell.

The built sky consists of ordered luminous regions, interrupted terraces, deep voids, and upward visual flow. The lower world is darker, heavier, and increasingly material.

The “engine” is not one giant machine. It is a distributed process embedded through the lower architecture.

## Six-frame journey

### Frame 1 — Constructed heaven

The upper two-thirds hold an immeasurably distant ordered interior surface. Thousands of restrained bone-white sources make it feel like a holy sky that was built.

The lower frame is black and sparse. The visitor cannot see a floor or complete boundary.

Everything in the composition appears to move upward, even while almost nothing actually moves.

### Frame 2 — Moving against the order

The visitor descends.

The luminous sky recedes overhead while large ceremonial levels enter the frame. These are not conventional stairs or mining terraces. They are interrupted processional planes, broad enough to hold architecture, void, and content.

The descent opposes the world’s apparent upward logic.

### Frame 3 — The processional levels

Each level is a content stop.

The levels differ by spatial function rather than colour or card styling. Some are wide and silent. Others are narrow transfer regions. Some disappear into black before reaching the frame edge.

Small luminous units or processed surfaces move upward in the distance. They remain ambiguous.

### Frame 4 — The discrepancy

At closer scale, the upward movement stops reading as ascension.

Material is being stripped, separated, reduced, cleaned, or reformatted as it rises. Dark residue falls or remains below. The orderly light overhead is the final state of this process.

Do not depict conveyor belts, pipes, cranes, workers, or recognisable factory equipment.

### Frame 5 — The lower truth

The visitor reaches the distributed machinery of the world.

It is filthy, decayed, and severe, but still abstract enough to belong only to Dark Lattice. There is no central engine, glowing reactor, furnace, or monster.

The visitor sees that the heaven above is assembled from processed output. What rises has had its previous state removed.

A visitor-selected unit, marked in the opening, can be traced through the process. Its history and final official form no longer match.

### Frame 6 — Return

The camera returns to the opening.

The built sky is still beautiful. The upward flow is still reverent.

Now every bright region reads as output, and every dark lower level reads as the cost that the opening concealed.

## Website content architecture

- **Opening:** Dark Lattice proposition.
- **Upper level:** Method and systems thesis.
- **Desk42 level:** Actual event, processing history, and official output.
- **Brawler level:** Propagation and neighbour consequences through one processing chain.
- **Labs:** This direction naturally supports sequential production levels. Use one piece of work moving through DesignLab, ArtLab, AudioLab, ContentLab, QALab, and ReleaseLab. Do not render six equal rooms or six cards.
- **Deep level:** Company, evidence boundary, and the hidden operational truth.
- **Return:** Contact and direct project routes.

## Interaction proposition

The visitor marks one luminous unit or makes one routing choice near the opening.

As the visitor descends, the selected unit travels upward through the opposite direction.

At the lower reveal, the visitor sees:

- its original state;
- what was removed;
- what was retained;
- what the constructed sky records.

This must remain a restrained causal thread, not a game mechanic.

## Visual grammar

- Upper world: bone, pearl-grey, ash-white.
- Lower world: graphite, carbon, dirty mineral neutral.
- No orange factory light.
- No purple ambience.
- No literal sacred ornament.
- Upward movement is sparse and slow.
- Atmosphere becomes denser only where it reveals distance and process.

## Automatic kill conditions

Kill H2 if:

- it reads as a dome, hangar, cooling tower, mine, temple, factory, or spaceship interior;
- a central machine becomes the reveal;
- the upward motion is only decorative;
- the lower levels look like generic industrial concept art;
- each content stop becomes a themed room;
- the sky and lower process do not clearly belong to one world;
- the mobile version becomes a cropped vertical fly-through.

# H3 — THE IMPOSED FIELD

## Governing sentence

**The beautiful sky is a classification system holding every entry in an assigned position.**

The visitor first reads the field as a holy star map. The exact spacing, fractures, gaps, and hidden supports gradually reveal that it is a record under constraint.

## Why it belongs to Dark Lattice

This is the most directly specific to Desk42 and the studio’s reality-versus-record philosophy.

Its challenge is spectacle. It must feel world-scale and material, not like a data visualisation, LED wall, cemetery, starfield, or installation.

## World architecture

A vast three-dimensional field of pale luminous structures extends beyond every frame boundary.

Do not use dots, stars, tombstones, identical monoliths, or line-grid markers.

Each entry should be a substantial ambiguous material form:

- thin faceted plates;
- fractured vertical seams;
- suspended structural witnesses;
- partially occluded masses with light escaping through damaged interfaces.

The forms differ enough to feel material, but their positions obey an exact hidden order.

## Six-frame journey

### Frame 1 — Holy field

A near-black void contains an immense ordered field.

From a distance, the luminous forms read like a sacred celestial map. The field has no visible edge, floor, roof, or complete boundary. Large black voids preserve scale and typography.

### Frame 2 — Parallax of assignment

The visitor approaches and the field separates into deep layers.

The spacing is too exact. Different-looking entries occupy mathematically consistent positions. Some locations are empty.

The gaps initially read as elegant composition.

### Frame 3 — Descent through the register

The visitor moves between entries at building scale.

Project and company content occupies cleared voids, not panels. The forms reveal fractures, dark contact points, and restrained material weight.

The field remains quiet. There is no particle storm or constant drift.

### Frame 4 — The discrepancy

Light escapes only through damaged interfaces.

Very faint constraint relationships become legible through shadow alignment, occlusion, or structural support. Avoid drawing a visible wireframe grid.

The empty positions are too exact to be natural. Entries have been removed.

### Frame 5 — The imposed lattice

Below and behind the luminous field, a dead graphite structure becomes visible.

It does not glow. It holds every entry in an assigned position.

Removed entries are not dramatically discarded. Their remains appear in compacted archival strata or dark storage depth. The beautiful field is an imposed record.

A visitor-disturbed entry has been corrected, moved, or removed. Its absence becomes evidence.

### Frame 6 — Return

The opening field appears again.

It remains cosmic and holy, but the eye now sees the hidden spacing law, the damaged light, and the exact gaps.

The visitor can identify the location where their entry used to be.

## Website content architecture

- **Opening:** Dark Lattice proposition.
- **Method:** How rules, records, and consequences shape systems.
- **Desk42:** Native fit. Show the actual event, official entry, correction, and retained absence through real project evidence.
- **Brawler:** One displaced entry forces neighbouring reassignment or propagates instability through the field.
- **Labs:** Follow one entry through rule definition, presentation, audio state, content, verification, and release. Do not turn the field into six labelled columns.
- **Company and contact:** The field recedes and the editorial layer becomes calmer.

## Interaction proposition

The visitor displaces one entry.

The field responds by:

1. attempting to restore its assigned position;
2. propagating corrections through nearby entries;
3. preserving a visible trace of the original displacement;
4. possibly removing the entry when it cannot be reconciled.

The visitor later sees the gap or corrected record.

## Visual grammar

- Forms: graphite, ash, bone, damaged off-white.
- Light: only through strain, fracture, or imposed contact.
- No stars, sparkle effects, dot grids, or constellations.
- No explicit matrix lines.
- No cemetery symbolism.
- No identical repeated primitive.
- Cyan may identify the visitor’s actual displaced state. It must not become ambient colour.

## Automatic kill conditions

Kill H3 if:

- it reads as a starfield, cemetery, LED installation, data visualisation, matrix, gallery, or generative grid;
- repeated forms become immediately nameable;
- the hidden lattice must be explained by copy before it can be seen;
- the field lacks material scale;
- the opening cannot compete emotionally with H1 and H2;
- the interaction becomes snapping or generic rearrangement.

| Direction | Strongest quality | Main risk | Best reason to continue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H1 — The Load** | Total reinterpretation through one lighting law | Generic glowing architecture | Every earlier frame inverts without adding a new reveal object |

## Gate 1 — Two-frame kill test for all three

For H1, H2, and H3 create:

1. Opening frame.
2. Reveal frame.

Same resolution. Same level of finish. Same typography discipline.

Required variants:

- full treatment;
- grayscale;
- bloom disabled;
- fog reduced;
- text-safe-zone overlay.

Show the images without concept names or explanations.

1. What are you looking at?
2. What industry could own this?
3. What feels holy?
4. What feels generic?

# Founder override

No metric, automated score, shader measurement, test result, or agent opinion can overrule Jacob’s visual judgement.

When Jacob says a frame looks wrong:

1. describe what is literally visible;
2. classify the defect as structural or cosmetic;
3. attempt one targeted correction only when the concept was not honestly exercised;
4. otherwise kill the direction;
5. do not defend sunk cost.

No tool guarantees a flawless website. This system prevents Claude from spending production time on an unapproved image and gives every stage a visible acceptance boundary.
