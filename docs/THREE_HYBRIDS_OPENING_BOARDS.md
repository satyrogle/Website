# H1 / H2 / H3 — OPENING REFERENCE BOARDS

**Scope:** the opening frame only, one per concept. Photographic and cinematic
references exclusively — things a camera actually recorded, because that is what
produces quality and what the eye trusts.
**Status:** three candidates at equal standing. No winner.
**Rule:** TAKE the quality, REJECT the literal thing. Nothing is copied.

---

# H1 — THE LOAD
*Light is the visible cost of holding the structure together.*

**1. Dune: Part Two — Giedi Prime, infrared monochrome** *(Greig Fraser)*
- TAKE — monochrome that is not grey: blown white against absolute black, almost no midtone. Colossal unornamented mass. Scale stated without a single human cue.
- REJECT — the arena bowl, the crowd, the black sun, the fireworks.
- FRAME — opening grade and value structure. This is the register.

**2. Blade Runner 2049 — Wallace Corporation interiors** *(Roger Deakins)*
- TAKE — an enormous volume lit by a source that is never shown; mass reading through near-darkness; confidence to leave most of the frame unresolved.
- REJECT — the rippling water ceiling, the amber palette, any room that closes.
- FRAME — opening. How to light something colossal without revealing the lamp.

**3. Ship hull on keel blocks, dry dock** *(documentary photography)*
- TAKE — tens of thousands of tonnes carried on a sparse line of small contacts. Everything that matters happens where mass meets support. The supports look undersized.
- REJECT — the hull silhouette, the yard, cranes, sodium lighting, scale figures.
- FRAME — opening. This is the load law as a real photograph.

**4. Photoelastic stress fringes** *(laboratory photography)*
- TAKE — light intensity as a direct readout of internal force; brightness crowding tightest at contact points; unloaded material simply dark.
- REJECT — the rainbow spectrum, banned outright by constitution 4.4. The bench, the transparent plastic, the diagram read.
- FRAME — opening. The physical justification for why anything is bright.

**5. Edward Burtynsky — quarry walls, large format**
- TAKE — cut mass at territorial scale; something enormous and clearly made that continues past every frame edge.
- REJECT — the quarry itself, which is a kill word. Daylight, colour, machinery, and any element that gives away the real size.
- FRAME — opening. Mass that reads as terrain-sized without being terrain.

**6. Hiroshi Sugimoto — Theatres**
- TAKE — absolute tonal control: one luminous region held perfectly against blacks that still carry information. Nothing is clipped by accident.
- REJECT — the screen, the proscenium, the symmetry, minimalism as a subject.
- FRAME — opening. The exposure discipline.

**7. Interstellar — Endurance against Gargantua** *(Hoyte van Hoytema)*
- TAKE — a thing dwarfed by something whose edge is never shown; scale by absence of boundary.
- REJECT — space, stars, the spacecraft, the accretion disc, the planet.
- FRAME — opening. Why nothing may be fully framed.

**8. Gravity dam downstream face, floodlit at night**
- TAKE — a single mass so large the available light reaches only part of it, the rest inferred from where the light stops.
- REJECT — water, the dam read, the civil-engineering context, safety lighting.
- FRAME — opening. Partial illumination of an enormous continuous surface.

# H2 — THE ASCENSION ENGINE, INSIDE
*The heaven above is manufactured output.*

**1. Super-Kamiokande photomultiplier array**
- TAKE — thousands of identical ordered units forming a surface that curves away and is never seen whole. At that count, regularity stops reading as pattern and starts reading as sky.
- REJECT — the gold and bronze, the cylindrical tank, the water, the boat, the facility. Crop so no curve completes.
- FRAME — opening. The built sky.

**2. 2001: A Space Odyssey — TMA-1 excavation** *(Kubrick / Unsworth)*
- TAKE — floodlit ordered geometry in a black pit; the sense of something uncovered rather than decorated; light with an industrial reason.
- REJECT — the monolith, the suits, the ramp, the sun cresting.
- FRAME — opening and the lower world.

**3. Piranesi — Carceri d'Invenzione**
- TAKE — an interior too large to measure; levels above levels; structures leaving every edge; no wall that resolves the volume.
- REJECT — the etched line quality, chains, wheels, winches, staircases, the prison.
- FRAME — opening. The reference that prevents a dome.

**4. Almería greenhouse complex from the air at night**
- TAKE — a made sky of thousands of countable light sources seen through atmospheric depth; brightness organised into regions rather than scattered.
- REJECT — agriculture, the ground plane, recognisable geography, warm light, roads.
- FRAME — opening.

**5. Continuous casting hall at night, long exposure monochrome**
- TAKE — material visibly being transformed; an ordered process legible without any single machine being identifiable.
- REJECT — every recognisable machine, conveyor and crane. The orange heat. Workers. The factory read the kit kills by name.
- FRAME — the lower world, informing what the opening's sky is made from.

**6. Anselm Kiefer — lead and ash works**
- TAKE — stratified material poverty: ash, lead, sediment, matte surfaces that hold no light of their own.
- REJECT — paint texture, canvas, the gallery, straw, figurative or textual elements.
- FRAME — the lower world.

**7. Arrival — the shell interior** *(Bradford Young)*
- TAKE — a volume whose boundary never resolves; scale established purely by the absence of any reference object.
- REJECT — the heptapods, the fog wall, the gravity flip, the white void.
- FRAME — opening.

**8. Andreas Gursky — industrial interiors**
- TAKE — obsessive ordered repetition at vast scale, photographed with no hierarchy, every unit equally sharp.
- REJECT — colour, the human world, retail, any subject that dates it.
- FRAME — opening. How order at scale is photographed.

# H3 — THE IMPOSED FIELD
*The beautiful sky is a classification system holding every entry in an assigned position.*

**1. Geological core archive racks**
- TAKE — thousands of materially varied samples in exact assigned positions. Position carries meaning the object does not. A register, not a collection.
- REJECT — shelving, racking, the warehouse, labels, barcodes, the room.
- FRAME — opening. This is the concept's spine.

**2. Michael Heizer — City, aerial photography**
- TAKE — unornamented imposed geometry at territorial scale; mass that refuses to explain itself.
- REJECT — the desert, daylight, the earthwork read, the horizon.
- FRAME — the lattice beneath.

**3. Delaminated mica, macro photography**
- TAKE — the entry form itself: a thin substantial plate with real thickness, light escaping only where the layers have separated.
- REJECT — the mineral-specimen photograph, the collector's context, gem-like clarity, warm transmitted colour.
- FRAME — opening. What a single entry is made of.

**4. Dune: Part Two — Harkonnen monochrome** *(Greig Fraser)*
- TAKE — pale forms against absolute black with almost no midtone; material that reads as manufactured rather than natural.
- REJECT — the figures, the arena, the black sun, the water.
- FRAME — opening grade.

**5. Fractured laminated glass, held, backlit**
- TAKE — internal fracture that stays contained; light finding the fracture and nothing else; damage as the only reason a thing is visible.
- REJECT — the window, the frame, the impact starburst, the shattered-screen cliché.
- FRAME — opening detail and the discrepancy.

**6. Container terminal from above at night, monochrome long exposure**
- TAKE — exact positional order holding varied units at territorial scale, with gaps where units have been removed. The gaps are too exact to be accidental.
- REJECT — containers, ships, cranes, colour, the logistics read, anything nameable.
- FRAME — opening. Order plus removal, photographed.

**7. Andreas Gursky — Rhein II / 99 Cent**
- TAKE — vast ordered repetition with no hierarchy and no focal point; every part equally resolved; the viewer given no place to rest.
- REJECT — colour, retail, the horizontal band, the human world.
- FRAME — opening composition.

**8. Herbarium type specimens, one withdrawn**
- TAKE — absence as evidence rather than emptiness; the difference between nothing being there and something having been removed.
- REJECT — paper, mounts, handwriting, the archival aesthetic entirely.
- FRAME — the reveal, and why the gaps must already be in the opening.
