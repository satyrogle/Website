# V3 — interior reference candidates

Gate V3 of `THE_CROSSING_PLAN.md`. Three interior spatial grammars, one
per file, full frame, 16:9, no grid, no collage, no text in the picture.
Jacob generates them in his image tool and picks ONE. The picked file
becomes implementation truth for I3; the other two are recorded and
dropped.

Nothing in I3 begins before a pick, because every direction this project
has ever taken from prose has collapsed on first render.

---

## What all three must obey

The visitor has just passed through the lit seam of a black monument and
is now inside it. The inside is impossibly larger than the outside: this
is the second awe beat, and it has to land on arrival.

- Near-black. One cold blue light family. Sparse warm-white points.
- No human-scale architecture anywhere. No doors, rails, walkways,
  platforms, panels, machinery, signage or vehicles. Nothing that
  implies a body.
- One vast continuous spatial field, a real horizon, and enormous empty
  intervals. Emptiness is the subject as much as the structure is.
- A few immense changes of level. Never repeated steps or terraces.
- Sparse small lights lying in the space - individually placed, widely
  separated, cold. They are records, not stars and not dust. There is no
  glowing fog, no particle haze, no bokeh.
- Enough mid-value structure across the middle distance that a huge
  moving shadow could later be seen crossing it. Not a flat black plane
  and not a silhouette against a bright sky.
- No focal object in the centre. No tower, no orb, no portal, no ring,
  no figure, no creature.
- Atmosphere present but thin: air with depth in it, not smoke.

Banned outright: terraces everywhere, a flat black plane with glitter, a
city, a canyon, a data visualisation, particle weather, modular sci-fi
platforms, generic game-engine concept terrain, cathedral interiors,
cave systems with stalactites.

## Candidate A — THE BASIN

> Interior of an immense dark chamber, photographed from ground level.
> The floor is one continuous near-black surface that curves gently
> upward toward the edges of vision, so the ground rises away in every
> direction and the horizon sits high in the frame. The surface is dry,
> matte, faintly fractured stone, unbroken for enormous distances. A
> handful of small cold white points lie on it, widely scattered, very
> far apart, each tiny and sharp with no glow around it. The air is
> thin and cold with real depth in it. Almost all of the frame is
> near-black; the light that exists is concentrated in a narrow band far
> away where the curve of the floor catches it. No structures, no
> objects, no figures, no centre of interest. Cinematic wide lens, still,
> severe, enormous scale. 16:9.

The grammar: **containment as a vessel.** Depth comes from the curve of
the ground. Everything stands on one continuous surface, so a shadow
later crossing it is unambiguous.

## Candidate B — THE UNDERSIDE

> Interior of an immense dark space, photographed from ground level
> looking slightly upward. Far overhead, filling the top two thirds of
> the frame, is the underside of a vast structure - a rough, sectioned
> ceiling of dark stone with immense flat facets and deep shadowed
> recesses, receding into the distance until it merges with the dark.
> Below it a nearly featureless black floor runs to a low horizon. Thin
> shafts of cold blue-white light come down through a few narrow gaps in
> the ceiling, far apart, landing as small pale patches on the floor.
> Most of the frame is near-black. No walls are visible on either side.
> No structures on the ground, no objects, no figures. Cold, still,
> oppressive scale. Cinematic wide lens. 16:9.

The grammar: **containment as a lid.** Depth comes from the ceiling's
recession. This is the interior answer to the sky's lid, and the light
already has an honest source.

## Candidate C — THE DROP

> Interior of an immense dark space, photographed from ground level. The
> near ground is a flat black plain that ends abruptly a long way ahead
> at the edge of an enormous drop - a sheer dark cliff face hundreds of
> times human height, running left to right across the whole frame and
> falling away into darkness. Beyond and far below, another vast plain
> continues to a distant horizon, and beyond that the faint edge of a
> second drop. The cliff faces are rough fractured stone, lit only by a
> grazing cold light that picks out one face and leaves the rest black.
> A few tiny cold white points lie on the lower plain, extremely far
> apart. Most of the frame is near-black. No structures, no objects, no
> figures, no stairs or paths of any kind. Cinematic wide lens, still,
> geological, immense. 16:9.

The grammar: **containment as depth.** Depth comes from two or three
continental level changes. It gives the descent somewhere to actually
go, and it is the one candidate where the shadow road would run over an
edge and reappear below it.

---

## How to judge them

Not by which is prettiest. By which one:

1. still reads as INSIDE something, not as an outdoor landscape;
2. could hold four content stops without becoming scenery behind text;
3. gives a moving shadow something to cross that would be visible;
4. ends somewhere - has a place the descent is heading toward;
5. carries no human scale anywhere in it.

One image per file. No sheet.

# PICKED, 2026-08-23: THE APERTURE

None of the three. Jacob's own generations produced a fourth grammar
that beats all of them, and the pick is his image of a fissure interior
looking OUT.

**The frame:** the visitor stands inside the crack. Two rock walls fill
the left and right of the frame, each with a blade of brilliant white
down its edge - the seam they just came through, seen from inside. The
gap between them opens onto an immense dark plain running to a hazed
horizon, with a few low flat slab islands in the middle distance and
sparse cold lights lying on the ground: some points, some short dashes.

**Why it wins.** The Drop failed exactly one test - it did not read as
inside anything - and the aperture fixes that without a ceiling, because
the walls of the crack are in shot. It keeps everything the Drop won on:
a real country with somewhere to go, slab islands for the stations to
stand on, and a huge flat surface for a shadow to cross. And the entry
lights already read as records rather than stars, because they lie ON
the ground with dashes among them instead of hanging in a sky.

**Also taken, for I4:** the aerial frame of a broad soft-edged shadow
wedge lying diagonally across a plated plain with thin light seams
running through it. That is the shadow road, and it is the reference
gate V4 was going to ask for.

**Also taken, for the corrosion:** the two macro stone frames - one
dominant diagonal band of vesicular pitting on a clean black majority.
That is the hierarchy E0.3 built, confirmed as a material target.

**Refused, with reasons.** Green glowing veins: radioactive green is
banned by the colour grammar and it reads as generic sci-fi. The rust
and ochre corrosion: caramel crust is banned, and the palette is black,
bone and one cold event colour. The studio-lit blocks on a fog stage:
that is a product render, an object beside copy with the copy left off.
The stepped ziggurat masses: that is the podium language E0 just
deleted, and it cannot come back as the interior's grammar.

**Carried in as hard constraints, learned from the misses:** no
symmetric vanishing-point composition anywhere - that is how the
Underside variants became a corridor. And the entry lights stay sparse
and unevenly clustered; the moment they space out evenly they are a
starfield, which is a paid-for kill word.
